Onsite — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Onsite ("the app") is a scheduling and client-management app for mobile service professionals (hairstylists, massage therapists, groomers, trainers, and similar businesses). This policy explains what data the app touches, why, and where it goes.

The short version: Onsite stores your business data on your own device. There is no Onsite server, no operator account, and no analytics or advertising SDK. Addresses and map coordinates are sent to free map services only when you use address, drive-time or "On My Way" features.

Who this covers

This policy is written for the operator — the person who installs Onsite to run their business. Onsite has no separate app or account for the operator's clients; clients are contacts the operator manages inside their own copy of the app.

Data Onsite accesses, and why

DataWhy Onsite asks for itWhere it goes
Contacts (read-only) One-time import of your phone contacts into your Onsite client list, only when you tap "Import from Contacts." Copied into the app's local database on your device. Never uploaded, never read again unless you re-import.
Calendar (read-only) Import personal events from your device calendar so Onsite can show them alongside your bookings and avoid scheduling over them. Only requested when you tap "Import." Copied into the app's local database. Onsite never writes to or modifies your calendar.
Location (foreground only) If you turn on GPS auto-complete, Onsite checks your position once when the app is open and marks an appointment complete if you are within about 150 metres of that client's address around the appointment time. Also an optional "On My Way" live ETA you start manually for a specific trip, and, when you choose "Current location" as the starting point, the route view and route planner. Used on-device. Onsite never collects location in the background. Coordinates for an active ETA trip are sent to a routing service, and a route you open in your maps app starts from where you are — see Third-Party Services below.
Notifications Appointment reminders. Generated and shown entirely on your device (local notifications).
Photos (optional) A profile photo for a client, if you choose one. Onsite never browses your photo library: the system photo picker hands over only the one picture you select. Shrunk and stored in the app's private storage on your device. Onsite never uploads it. It leaves the phone only inside a backup: one you export yourself, or your phone's own OS backup if you have it turned on.
Feedback (optional) If you tap "Send feedback", your email app opens with a draft that has the app version and your phone model. Nothing is sent by Onsite. You read the draft and press Send yourself, to our support address. No client or appointment information is added.

Onsite does not request background location, does not read your SMS messages, and does not read your contacts or calendar except during an import you explicitly trigger.

Data Onsite does NOT collect

  • No user accounts, no login, no password.
  • No analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising SDK.
  • No data is sold, rented, or used for advertising — Onsite shows no ads.
  • No client data is ever transmitted to a server operated by Onsite, because no such server exists. All appointment, client, and revenue records stay in a local database on your device.

Third-party services

Because Onsite has no server of its own, a few features call free, no-account, third-party services directly from your device:

  • OpenStreetMap (Nominatim / Photon) — turns an address you type into map coordinates, so the app can calculate drive times and show a pin. Only the address text is sent; no client name or other details.
  • OSRM (router.project-osrm.org) — calculates drive time/ETA between two points for the optional "On My Way" feature. Only two coordinate pairs are sent.
  • Your maps app (Google Maps, Waze, Apple Maps, or your phone's default) — tapping "Map" on an appointment opens the app you picked in Settings with that client's address. Opening a route hands Google Maps (or Apple Maps on iPhone) the start point and the stops' coordinates. This hands off to another app on your phone; Onsite has no maps API key and sends these companies no analytics. If you pick Waze and it isn't installed, the link opens Waze's website in your browser instead.
  • Your phone's own Messaging app — tapping a message action opens your phone's default SMS app with a prefilled message for you to review and send. Onsite never sends the message itself.

None of these services receive your name, your business identity, or your client's identity — only the specific coordinates or address text needed to answer that one request.

Smart Picks

Smart Picks suggests booking times using the appointments, saved locations and addresses already stored on your device. It adds no new data collection. Any address lookup it triggers goes to the map services listed above.

Where your data lives, and backup

All business data is stored in a local database on your device. Two things can copy it off the device, both under your control: your phone's own OS backup (Google Account backup / iCloud), if enabled, and a manual export you trigger yourself to a location you choose. Onsite has no way to access, read, or restore your data remotely.

Why Onsite does not rely on your phone's backup. Your phone's backup is worth having on, but it is not something you can count on. Google Account backup keeps at most 25 MB for each app. Once Onsite's data, including client photos, grows past that, Android stops backing it up and does not tell you. The backup still shows on your account and looks fine, but it holds an old copy, and you only find out when you need it. On iPhone, iCloud backup has no per-app limit, but it stops when your iCloud storage is full (Apple gives 5 GB free, shared with your photos). The phone shows a warning, but it is easy to miss. On both, the phone decides on its own when a backup runs, and phone backup does not cover files in Downloads or on an SD card.

The backup to rely on is the one Onsite makes: Settings › Your Data › Back up my data. It saves a complete copy of everything, at the moment you choose, to a place you choose, such as Google Drive, Dropbox or your email. You can see the file, so you know it is there.

Data deletion

Uninstalling Onsite deletes all locally stored data on that device. Because Onsite keeps no server-side copy, removing the app is the deletion. Backup files you exported, and your phone's own OS backups, stay where you saved them until you delete them.

Your choices and rights

You control your data on your device. To access, correct or delete it, use the app or uninstall it. We hold no copy, so we cannot look up, change or delete your data for you. Questions or requests about this policy: use the contact address below.

Children's privacy

Onsite is a business tool for adult service professionals and is not directed at, or knowingly used to collect data from, children.

Security

Onsite relies on your device's own operating-system security. Because there is no Onsite account or password, an optional App Lock feature (off by default) can require your phone's biometric or a PIN before the app's contents are shown.

Changes to this policy

If what Onsite collects or how changes, this page will be updated and the "Last updated" date above will change accordingly.
